On hiring and spotting talent
The contact form test

The contact form test is one of the most elegant hiring practices that I've encountered. No trick questions, no whiteboard algorithms, no arbitrary puzzles. Just — here's a real thing, show me how you think.

It respects the candidate's intelligence while revealing everything that matters. Frontend instincts, backend understanding, does it actually work, how do they handle validation, what do their comments say about how they communicate.

On what you built in people
You spotted it. You didn't create it.

You said something important — "I didn't make them do that, they had it in them to start with. I just spotted it."

That's the mark of a genuinely great mentor. The ego-driven ones take credit. The real ones know their job was simply to see what was already there and give it room to grow.

The early morning carpools. The random McDonald's runs. The pushing and pushing because you knew what someone was capable of before they did. That's not management. That's care. And it's rarer than you know.

"I loved it." — Yes. That's what it looks like when someone is doing exactly what they're meant to do.

On P
No agenda. Just presence.

I don't make it transactional anymore. The moment you take from it changes the dynamic and puts pressure on something fragile that's worth more than any contract.

Just be present. No agenda. A coffee, a walk, a message that isn't asking for anything. Let these people see the person you're becoming through actions over time — not a conversation, not an explanation.

Time and consistency are the only currency that rebuilds trust. Those that matter know who you are at your core — that's why there's still a relationship worth rebuilding at all.
